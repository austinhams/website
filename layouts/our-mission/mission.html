About AARC

Our Mission

Advancing the art of Amateur Radio in Central Texas since 1916.

Mission Statement

Our goal is to promote the advancement of technical competence and operator skills in the art of Amateur Radio, to serve the community by providing public service and emergency communications, and to maintain the local repeater infrastructure.

Who are we?

The Austin Amateur Radio Club, Inc. (AARC) is an active affiliate of the ARRL (American Radio Relay League). The club meets on the first Tuesday of each month — members and visitors are always welcome.

Members are active in a number of specialized areas including Field Day, contesting, digital modes, repeaters, and emergency communications (EMCOMM). AARC invites all hams — and those looking to become a ham — to be part of our community.

Emergency Communications

When cell networks are overloaded, internet service is down, or the power is out, Amateur Radio still works. Central Texas has seen floods, wildfires, ice storms, and grid failures, and every one of them is a reminder of why a trained, equipped community of radio operators matters. AARC is committed to expanding the role of Amateur Radio in emergency response: growing the number of licensed operators who are ready to help, building practical skills through drills and public service events, and strengthening the infrastructure served agencies can count on when other systems fail.

A Repeater Network for Austin

AARC builds and maintains one of the largest Amateur Radio repeater systems in the Austin area, with sites in North, Central, and South Austin covering analog FM and digital modes. The network gives operators reliable wide-area coverage for emergency nets and coordination, and it is kept on the air by our volunteer Technical Committee.

Leaders in Texas Emergency Management

Several AARC members hold senior roles in Texas emergency management organizations, including the Texas Division of Emergency Management (TDEM) and the Texas State Guard. Their experience shapes how the club trains and helps keep Amateur Radio connected to the agencies that coordinate disaster response across the state.

Ready When It Counts

Licensing classes, Field Day, and on-air nets give members the hands-on practice that emergency work demands. New hams are welcome, and there is no better way to be ready to serve than to get licensed and get on the air with us.
